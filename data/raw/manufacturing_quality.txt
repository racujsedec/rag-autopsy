Crestline Motors reported an increase in paint-surface defects on one assembly line. The defect increase was linked to elevated humidity in the paint booth and a delay in replacing intake filters. Robot calibration measurements remained within tolerance, and inspection data did not support the initial suspicion that robotic positioning caused the problem. The issue was concentrated on shifts with the highest booth humidity.

Maintenance replaced the filters and tightened humidity alarms for the booth. Defect rates returned to the prior-month range after those controls were introduced. Quality engineering also added humidity and filter-age fields to the daily defect review so that environmental conditions can be compared with surface-quality results before defect levels become significant.
