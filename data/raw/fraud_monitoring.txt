Harbor Bank's fraud-monitoring team investigated a sudden increase in alerts for card purchases. The spike was caused by merchant-category drift that pushed legitimate travel purchases above a risk threshold. Chargeback volume remained stable, and the team did not see a corresponding increase in confirmed fraud. A recent model deployment was reviewed, but its feature calculations were operating as designed.

Analysts recalibrated the affected threshold and added a monitoring rule for abrupt changes in merchant-category distributions. They also created a weekly comparison between alert rates and confirmed fraud outcomes so that rising false-positive rates could be separated from genuine changes in fraud activity. Customer declines decreased after the threshold adjustment.
